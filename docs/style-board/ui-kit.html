Taterland UI kit

Reference mockups for Segment 21h, tokens brightened for the vibrancy pass (21i F). Codex matches these side by side, screen for screen. Every colour and face on this page is the game's own; nothing here is a suggestion.

The three rules under every mockup: ink felt is the ground, paper carries facts, wood frames. One drawing above every name. No grey, no white, no cream on cream, no text under 14 px on a phone.

Everything about one thing shares one colour, everywhere it appears. Golden is gold on its seed card, its stamp, its sale line and its catalogue entry. Each keeper owns a colour used on their page header, portrait frame and Winter lines.

Two typefaces, pick the display face

One display face for names, numbers and signs; one plain body face. The five bundled fonts go. Three display candidates below; the body face is the same in all three. Pick on a phone, then it is fixed for the whole game.

Nell's books · 104,000C. Gaegu bold. Handwritten with a pen. Loosest, friendliest, least sign-like.

Body in all three: Atkinson Hyperlegible, 16 px, with 14 px captions and 22 px headings. Nothing smaller on a phone.

Quiet pages, one pop

Tester 1: "too many things pop out." Every page has one theme colour, its keeper's or its place's, used quietly, and exactly one element at full chroma: the thing to tap next. A result may add a second pop when it lands. Everything else is paper, ink and muted. Squint at the page: if more than two things still stand out, it is wrong.

How to apply it: theme tint = the page colour mixed 30% into paper for the header, 40% for the selected row's edge. Full chroma only on the primary button, a landed result, or a selected item. Potato colours on a board are thin lines and small drawings, not fills. Keeper headers lose their saturated fill and keep their colour as the tint and the portrait frame.

A superstition is a passive

Balatro's jokers are easy because they are passive: you own it, it works, one line says what it does, and it looks funny. A superstition is the same thing. One drawing with a face, a name of at most three words, one effect line of at most eight words with a number or a plain rule in it. No "takes effect next Winter", no hooks to hang, nothing to switch on. Every superstition you own works from the moment its card lands.

When one fires, the thing it touched shows the card's drawing, its name and its number for one second, at the place it happened and one at a time: Lucky Boot ×2 over the bed, Cold Store · kept on the stores line. Never a running total, never a digit on the HUD. The reveal card (a pack result) adds the folk line above the effect, as the cause: "Nobody put it there." then "No pests, ever." Collection and gate cards show the name and the effect only.

Fennick's cart

Three packs on a shelf, drawn as things, not columns. Name and price under each and nothing else, until you pick one up. Tap a pack and it lifts and turns: the odds are printed on its back, with the one Buy button. Tap outside and it goes back. The Mystery pack wobbles once in a while and is the only thing that moves. A sold pack lies flat, muted, and reads Sold.

The market board

All five potatoes at once, one row each, on Mara's Sell page and on the pier board. A row is a drawing, the name, how many tonnes you hold, a jagged 90-second line, and the current price with the last tick's arrow. Tap a row to pick it; one sell bar at the bottom serves the picked row. No button inside a row, no page per potato. A boom stamps its multiplier beside the name.

What gets a screen sent back

Grey or white anywhere. Ink, paper, wood only.
A word pill where a picture fits. Hats, shirts, tools, crops, keepers are drawn.
Text under 14 px at 390 wide, or more than three sizes on a screen.
A scrollbar on a card. Cards fit; pages scroll.
A rarity word or star. The edge colour is the rarity.
A third typeface.
A keeper line out of voice or longer than eight words.
A preview that crops the farmer.
More than two things that pop on one page when you squint.

# Rook's capsule counter — proposal

This is a design proposal for discussion. v1.0.3.1 adds activity XP and a 10%
Build Crate chance; it retains the current Roll House and harvest stakes.

## Suggested design

Sell Taterland once on Steam and keep all capsule access earned through play.
Rook runs a theatrical cabinet of potato-shaped capsules, with the red/gold
curtains and lights framing a physical capsule reveal.

- Earn non-purchasable tickets from island quests and farming milestones.
  Do not sell tickets, ticket currency, random-reward DLC or paid ways to
  replenish attempts.
- One ticket opens one capsule containing an item. Remove empty rewards,
  betting amounts, all-in, stake refunds and Spudion jackpots.
- Keep each island's collectible pool appropriate to its economy, with rare
  artifacts and island wonders. Show the actual odds and contents before
  opening.
- Convert duplicates to collection stamps redeemable for a chosen item.
  Show guaranteed-reward progress and preserve it through saves. Exact odds
  and guarantee thresholds need balancing before implementation.
- Keep capsule rewards and stamps non-tradable, with no cash-out or Steam
  Community Market connection. They should not become currency for more bets.
- Give every base build a deterministic unlock milestone. Activity XP remains
  the normal leveling route, with the requested 10% Build Crate chance carried
  over to capsule rewards as a bonus.
- Replace the Gambler's crop-staking activity as well: Collector could complete
  collection requests, earn stamps and improve reward choice. Preserve existing
  Gambler levels and XP during that migration.
- Replace the slot-style reel with a capsule opening. Keep rarity reveals,
  character reactions and distinct artifact animations.

## Why the mechanic matters

Steam requires the Content Survey before store/build review and compares the
answers with the submitted product. Complete it for the final design; a new
name does not establish a rating or guarantee approval.
[Steam Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)

Australia's current rules assign R18+ to simulated gambling and a minimum M
to in-game purchases linked to chance. Its FAQ says that changing the type of
currency does not alter the simulated-gambling test, and indirect real-money
purchases still count for paid random rewards. Even a small gambling activity
inside a larger farming game can be relevant.
[Classification overview](https://www.classification.gov.au/about-us/media-and-news/news/new-classifications-for-gambling-content-video-games),
[Australian Classification FAQ, hosted by IGEA](https://igea.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/INFRA6558-Australian-Classifications-%E2%80%93-Fact-sheet-%E2%80%93-industry-FAQs_v6.pdf)

An earned collectible-capsule system would change the design substantially.
Its final classification still depends on the implemented mechanics,
presentation and intended territories; this proposal is not a classification
decision. The most conservative alternative is to let players choose every
quest reward directly and keep only the reveal animation random.

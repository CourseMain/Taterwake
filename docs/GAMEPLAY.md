# Taterland gameplay guide

**A farming game where you lose money.** This describes the local v2.0.3 redesign. The public browser game remains v2.0.1 while publishing is paused.

## Keep the farm for ten years

Every launch opens at the wooden TATERLAND gate in golden-hour light. The camera pulls back for eight seconds, then drifts. A fresh farm has one large **Walk to the farm** button and fits the island to the current screen. One optional farmer card lets you choose a hat, shirt colour, skin tone and name, or Skip. The first guide follows. A saved farm offers **Continue · Year N, Season**, plus **Start a new farm** with a confirmation that defaults to **Keep my farm**. Waiting at the gate does not advance the year.

You inherit a Spud Valley farm with **80,000 Spudions**, a **480,000 mortgage**, twelve open Home Field beds, twelve more available through expansion, and two rentable fields. Your purse is opening cash plus every entry in the ledger. A harvest is not income until someone pays for it.

Spring is for planting, Summer for tending, Autumn for harvesting, and Winter for accounts and protection work. Each season lasts **150 seconds**; a farm shop reduces Summer to **120 seconds**. Winter accounts pause time. Close them to clear ice and prepare for Spring. The top row shows the season and year and larger outlined money on transparent backgrounds. Its solid yellow potato Spudion has two eyes and a highlight and gently moves. Menu is a drawn notebook on a rounded wood tile, and Weather shows a live sun, cloud, rain, storm or snow drawing with a warning dot. Captions sit under the drawings; Sell shows a sack and Tools the equipped tool. Fullscreen sits at the upper left, clear of the calendar. Tap the season to open the calendar. Saves preserve the farm across sessions, with no offline growth.

The guided year shows one instruction card at a time. Weather, Sell, prices and the calendar appear when first needed and stay available afterward. Meet Mara, use a starter Russet seed, then hoe, plant and water the glowing bed. Decisions pause time; growing waits run at normal speed. Iris warns **“A small storm is coming”** for **30 seconds at normal speed**. Harvest the glowing bed before it arrives to avoid the demonstration loss. Otherwise the gust takes one of three tonnes and Tess explains it. Guided crop quality and pest damage are protected; seed costs, yield losses, sales, storage and bills remain real.

Harvest the demonstration crop, then **sell now or choose Store for Winter**. Bring in the other ripe starter beds too: the final guided wait pauses while any remain, so the guide cannot strand them in Autumn Cold. Nell opens the first Winter accounts and guidance ends. The full 104,000 fixed bills appear alongside one credit, **Year one covered by the last harvest**, for the same amount. Nell says: “Dad's last harvest paid this year. From now on it's yours.” This credit belongs only to the completed guided first year; skipping the guide or taking the optional tour earns none. Selling the starter harvest leaves opening cash plus those sales; optional purchases and storage still cost money. The gold cue follows your chosen bed; the correct tool is selected for each step. Blocked-action feedback appears below the instruction. **× → Skip guided year** leaves early without clearing hazards or refunding costs. Later help stays optional in **F1** or the farm menu. The optional Valley tour pauses and preserves the farm; it does not replay the introductory disaster.

After the accounts close, the Winter card separates **Winter · N jobs left** from **Stores**. Only iced beds, ripe Icecap, frost covers, paid project stages and available businesses count as jobs. Stores lists each held crop’s current and late-Winter quote, or **Stores · empty**. These are facts; selling and keeping seed happen at the barn. **Sleep until Spring** remains below the card and in Menu before the final Winter. Sleeping resolves weather before the ordinary Spring save and does not sell anything.

## Three fields, one island

Home, Low and Hill each contain 24 beds. Home starts with twelve open. Bram’s Tools shed opens the other twelve for **48,000 in any season**; the card says so. Locked Home beds point there with the price. Locked Low and Hill beds name their own Winter lease and annual price. Rent Low or Hill from the **Winter accounts** to open its first twelve; each field's second half costs **48,000**. The two leases renew each Winter, posted under Rent. Cancellation refunds that Winter's renewal, removes standing crops and closes its beds; purchased expansions remain if you rent again.

| Field | Annual rent | Exposure |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Home · Sheltered | Included in fixed costs | Storm and flood ×0.8 |
| Low · Floods first | 55,000 | Yield ×1.25; flood ×1.5; drought ×0.8 |
| Hill · Dries first | 9,000 | Drought ×1.5; flood ×0.5; storm ×1.3; freeze ×1.2 |

Exposure multiplies weather stress and resulting loss. Protection projects share their levels across the farm; Spring frost covers can be placed on any rented bed. Loss cards name the field. Low Field's extra whole tonnes are distributed across four beds, giving each complete half-field exactly 25% more. Ducks patrol open beds in all rented fields.

Walk, sprint or click around one continuous hexagonal Valley island with long angled coasts. Straight dirt lanes connect the village, field gates and pier in loops. Hill Field sits behind the village above three visible grass terraces, reached by one approach from the barn with four shallow treads per terrace flight, landings and handrails on both sides. Low Field lies closer to the village toward the front-right shore, beside reeds and a drainage ditch. A working orchard with a central lane, hedges and crates fills the west; a small compost and barrel yard sits to the east. All three fields use the same beds and soil. Low has a mild wet, green tint; Hill is paler and windswept. Field-name boards, exposure icons on their posts and gated fences mark the fields; unrented land is flat pale grass with up to three thin dry blades per bed and an overgrown TO LET board. The straighter overview keeps the whole island visible, and Home recentres it, including on a phone.

## Three decisions

### What to plant

Tap Mara’s stall to meet her, then choose **Buy seeds**. Her seed page has illustrated packets showing cost, yield, water needs, heat and cold tolerance, price volatility and seeds owned. Buy 1 and Buy 5 remain simple cash purchases. Packets swipe on phones. Mara has no Sell tab. Till a bed, select a seed, plant and water. Harvests enter the barn in **tonnes (t)**; sale prices are per tonne.

| Variety | Seed cost | Base price / tonne | Water need | Heat tolerance | Cold tolerance | Grow seasons | Tonnes / bed | Price swings |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Russet | 270 | 360 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 3 | Low |
| Giant | 360 | 480 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 5 | Low |
| Golden | 504 | 672 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 4 | Mid |
| Sunburst | 684 | 912 | 2 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 3 | High |
| Icecap | 1,200 | 1,200 | 3 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 2 | High |


More water bars mean thirstier crops; more heat or cold bars mean better tolerance. High prices come with vulnerabilities. Icecap’s two potential tonnes do not automatically make its expensive seed profitable. Till and plant ordinary crops in Spring and Summer. Icecap also plants into prepared Autumn beds and grows through Winter; clear its ice before working it. Other crops left in the field are lost at Winter’s start.

Each ordinary Spring opens one foldable **Spring plan** with three short rows: **Winter bills**, **Two plantings**, and **Short by**. Crop and bed changes update the estimate. Its tooltip identifies the crop and bed count and explains the healthy Table-grade, base-price assumption with bought seed deducted. Weather, storage and other purchases can lower the result. Soft leaf and blossom shades separate the rows, with a Spudion beside each amount. Hurry and its speed overlay have been removed. The guided year hides it.

Growth takes **60 / 90 / 110 / 160 / 200 seconds** for Russet / Golden / Giant / Sunburst / Icecap before weather slows it. Dry beds grow at **40%** of their watered speed and carry a clear droplet at the overview zoom; watering restores full speed without clearing progress. Harvested beds can be hoed and replanted immediately in the same Spring or Summer; two Russet harvests fit a calm season.

Crops have **Table, Standard or Feed** quality, selling for **1.2×, 1× or 0.5×** the quote. Dry soil, weather, pests and late harvesting reduce quality. Water promptly, clear ice, spray pests and bring ripe crops in. A full barn leaves excess yield on the plant. Each planting has one pest chance, scheduled between **25% and 60%** of its growth time: **16% normally, 24% in Summer, zero in Winter**. No first infestation starts after ripening. Pest attacks remove one third of the original yield every five seconds; spraying stops further loss. The first naturally appearing pest group after the guide is protected until cleared, then ordinary pest damage applies.

The first graded harvest opens one compact illustrated card: **Table — best, sells for more**, **Standard — normal**, **Feed — damaged, half price**. The first three harvest pops include their grade’s gloss; later pops show only the word. Harvest pops, inventory chips and barn sell rows share readable stamps at least 14 px wide-phone type, with warm gold, sage and clay fills. A harvest stamp holds for 1.2 seconds, then fades and passes taps through. Ripe potatoes occasionally wobble; harvesting makes three soft sparks and a two-note cue. Watering makes one bouncing drop. Growing-bed taps show large **Ready in N s** feedback. Autumn planting reminders appear only when attempted.

### When to sell

To sell harvested potatoes, walk to the **barn** or tap **Sell · Russet 360/t**. Both meet the same keeper before opening the same barn page. Choose a crop and grade, set an amount and confirm the sale. The page also handles Winter stores, kept seed and buyer orders. Standard quotes vary by ±5%, ±10% or ±15% with volatility. Only grades you hold have chips; their charts and amount preview show the actual price. Opening the barn never sells anything. Nell’s accounts have one **Go to the barn** link.

Sale charts zoom to the labelled recent-price range, so small rises and falls remain visible. A distant Winter target appears as a dashed edge marker with an arrow and its actual quote; it does not stretch the scale. Unchanged prices stay flat. Winter stores chart their own rising quotes rather than fresh-harvest prices. An ink outline marks which harvest view is selected.

Winter storage charges a flat **4,800**, spoils **5%** of held tonnes and removes **10 quality points** from survivors. An empty barn has no storage bill. These costs settle before foreclosure is checked. Winter-store prices rise from base toward **1.2× for Low, 1.4× for Mid or 1.6× for High volatility**, multiplied by current grade. Weather can destroy stores before you sell. Spring resets the premium. Fresh Icecap harvested in Winter uses the ordinary quote.

Buyer orders are paper slips inside the barn. A **20-tonne Spring order** pays **1.1× base price**. At Winter’s start the buyer collects Standard or Table tonnes before storage costs and spoilage; each missing tonne costs **200**. Accepted orders cannot be cancelled. The Winter barn also shows each crop’s **keep up to N t** seed limit and one **Keep 1 t as seed** action. Each kept tonne becomes one seed next Spring, excluded from sale, ageing and spoilage.

Accepted orders have tagged crates beside the village road; a cart arrives for collection and leaves with the delivered sacks. In Winter, stored sacks stack inside the open barn, kept seed has its own crate, and a dark spoilage heap dwindles outside. These displays reflect the existing inventory and settlement; they do not change it.

### What to protect

Iris introduces each year’s weather page with a short, soft chirp and the climate record. After the guide, seasonal disaster chance starts at **15%**, adds four percentage points per year and caps at **60%**. Mean severity starts at **50%** and rises three points per year. At most one disaster occurs per season and three per year.

Spring grass stays green, and early Summer warms to dry green grass. Stronger Summer browning begins in year six. Spring brings flood or freeze; Summer drought or storm; Autumn storm or flood; Winter deep freeze or blizzard. Warnings last **45 seconds**, active danger **30**, then recovery **75**. Drier grass, rain and wind can hint at the next season, with false alarms and missed warnings. A better weather station narrows forecast uncertainty, not the weather itself. The forecast marks its probability range with a bracket on a 0–100% scale, alongside the station instrument. Iris’s year-start report is a newspaper front page with a weather column recording the ten years. Start the year stays reachable while the article scrolls.

Sky, grass and snow crossfade for one second at season boundaries. Two calm nature layers crossfade slowly, with breeze, leaves, a distant pond stream, light rain and Winter hush. Six bird calls vary with random 20–40-second gaps; Summer cicadas also come in bursts. Ambience stays below −24 dB. Quieter in Menu halves sound except tool cues. Wind peaks at −26 dB during the guide, −20 afterward; thunder is −18, pest cues −14 and tools −16. Three pest cues rotate at most once per 20 seconds. Alerts and charm sounds stay at least two seconds apart. Spring blossom, Summer heat shimmer, orange Autumn canopies and fallen leaves mark the seasons. Winter covers the island in continuous, softly rounded near-white snow and a few large, slowly drifting flakes. One visible sun disc and halo travel across a warmer patch of sky, above a hazy horizon. Spring and Summer rise from 20 to 35 degrees; Autumn and Winter rise from 14 to 27 degrees and use warmer sunlight. The direction eases to a new angle every three seconds. Stronger direct sunlight, modest ambient light and cool fill give roofs, fences, sails and posts a lit face and a shaded face. Shade is about 60–70% as bright as lit ground. Water and the steel tank catch the same sun. Terrace tops and shaded risers have different grass tones; Summer is straw-green over olive. Baked shading and soft contacts ground the trees, fences, buildings, villagers and inset soil beds. Growing potatoes read green; ripe tops show their variety’s potato colour. A deeper sky fades to a pale horizon, with faint haze over the far terrace. Coastal water fades from green-blue shallows to deep blue, with one pulsing foam edge, five broad drifting highlights and a low slow wave. Soft sunlight and pale-blue-grey shade shape the snow; Balanced and Crisp show soft, pale-blue cast sun shadows, while Smooth keeps the same lighting without cast shadows. Blizzards and deep freezes dim the light gently; recovery quickly restores it. The mill sails and scanning dish move without thin flickering shadows; their towers and bodies still cast shadows. Paths remain visible as compacted grey-white strips. Roofs, branches, rails and wall tops collect snow; eight small drifts sit in sheltered corners. The pond freezes beneath the ducks, with one snowman and a robin beside it. Fruit trees lose their leaves, tank water freezes, and walking leaves paired tracks that fade over a minute on snowy paths. Locked beds become plain snow patches. Only opened beds show soft snow furrows over a grey-white cracked glaze, which Hoe shatters. The farmer wears a Winter coat and hat, and a straw hat in Summer.

The water loop is **rain → tank → can or pipes → crops**. Tap the tank to refill. Sprinklers share its reserve; drought stops replenishment. Hoe clears crop ice or drains a flooded bed. Purchased drains can be opened and windbreaks reduce storm losses. Irrigation and optional paused water practice live at Iris’s weather station.

| Protection | Disaster | Level 1 | Level 2 |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Rainwater tank | Drought | 36,000 | 72,000 |
| Drainage | Flood | 48,000 | 96,000 |
| Windbreak | Storm | 60,000 | 120,000 |
| Frost cover | Spring freeze, covered beds only | 36,000 | 72,000 |


Reserve protection materials in Winter, then complete **three walked work actions** at the construction site per level. Paid unfinished work persists. Tank rings and scaffolding, drain sections, potted saplings and rolled covers show work in progress; completed tanks, channels, tree rows and pitched bed covers appear on the island. Completed levels reduce matching field yield loss by **50% or 75%**, with **2,400 annual upkeep per protection type**. Frost covers protect covered beds next Spring only: clear Winter ice, then use the **Winter jobs** cover line or the nearby cover action. Covers expire in Summer.

Spring crop insurance costs **9,600** and pays **40% of lost tonnes at base prices**. Subsequent field losses settle at Winter’s start; new Winter weather claims pay as they happen. Ordinary spoilage is excluded. Winter deep freeze and blizzard threaten stored crops and living Icecap. Selling stores or harvesting Icecap before impact reduces exposure.

Every loss has a **cause card**: the event, variety, tonnes lost, missing protection or action, and the tonnes that alternative would have saved. Tess, the quest keeper, keeps losses on her cork board with their base-price worth. That value is forgone crop, not another cash debit. Read the current year’s cards on **Tess’s board → Losses**; the annual accounts include their causes.

## The ledger

Nell keeps the accounts, readable in any season; leases and business plans remain Winter decisions. Year 1 states the 104,000 bills against about 45,000 at best from twelve Russet beds, with the three changes needed: more beds, a dearer potato and no empty Spring or Summer beds. These are plain directions to the farm’s services. The page has one **Go to the barn** link. **Mortgage and land · 60,000** opens interest 24,000, principal 24,000 and land tax 12,000. Underlying journal labels, categories and amounts are unchanged. The paper ledger shows the signed net, closed years, sales by grade, debt and climate record. Loss records belong to Tess’s board. **Save screenshot** writes a PNG and downloads it in the browser.

Every Winter posts these fixed bills once:

| Cost | Spudions per year |
| --- | ---: |
| Mortgage interest | 24,000 |
| Mortgage principal | 24,000 |
| Rent and land tax | 12,000 |
| Living costs | 32,000 |
| Equipment upkeep | 12,000 |
| **Total** | **104,000** |


The guided first year alone also posts **Year one covered by the last harvest**, a 104,000 credit against the fixed bills. It is shown separately from other income and does not repeat in year two. Storage, insurance, protection upkeep, purchases and other transactions appear separately. Reloading cannot charge a settled bill again. Winter purchases remain in that year’s accounts.

The overdraft limit is **−200,000**. Edwin, the bank manager, appears when the purse falls **below −100,000**, more than halfway into it. He quotes the real balance and offers the same conversation choices as the other villagers. Purchases that would cross the overdraft limit are refused. At Winter settlement, income, claims and the bills settle before foreclosure is tested: **strictly below −200,000 ends the run; exactly −200,000 survives**.

From year three, the accounts offer a further decision: diversify or keep investing in crops and protection. Three compact tiles show each business’s effect and one build or enrol action; question marks explain timing and penalties.

| Business | Cost | Return and tradeoff |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Farm shop | 120,000 | 34,000 each Winter; Summer has 30 fewer seconds for farming. |
| Contract grower | Free enrolment | Two simultaneous Spring orders, each at 1.2× the ordinary contract price; normal shortfall penalties apply. |
| Lodging | 100,000 | 7,000 each Winter per completed protection type, up to 28,000. |


Business benefits begin the following year. Lodging counts completed tank, drainage, windbreak and frost-cover projects separately. Contract growers receive two simultaneous premium orders at base × 1.32; shortfall penalties still apply. These incomes and costs use the same ledger.

## Fifty years on

Survive the tenth Winter to read the earned title, final ledger and ten-box climate record. The summary keeps Nell’s portrait small, its total large and its epilogue lead-in above the fixed actions. Then the ending simulates **forty further years** from your final farm. A caretaker keeps your crop mix, protections and diversification, and repairs when affordable, while the climate curve continues.

Four scores—**solvency, adaptation, diversification and land health**—determine one of seven futures: **Dust, Drowned, Deserted, Sold to the estate, Holding on, The shop village or Thriving**. Thriving requires **every score above 0.75**. The same final state produces the same future.

The climate strip extends to fifty years with a headline for each decade. The camera fades up and slowly pans across the future farm: damaged beds, water and buildings show the result. Decade headlines fade in one at a time, then the four verdict lines type on, followed by **“Farm value, 50 years on”** and a screenshot button. Your playable ten-year save stays intact.

## Living in the Valley

Every service begins with its keeper: Mara at seeds, Nell at the barn or accounts, Bram at Tools, Iris at Weather, Tess at the board and Pip at ducks. A compact greeting offers one clear service button; personal stories remain optional. Close leaves the conversation. Service pages have one Close. Greetings change with the season and use your chosen name. A short pitched chirp plays once when a page opens, with no repeating voice per line. Farm notes hide during conversation. The farm clock pauses while the island continues moving. Fullscreen stays clear of page buttons.

Use WASD or arrows to walk and Shift to sprint. Click a bed to walk over and use the selected tool, or press E beside it. Tools are **1 Hoe, 2 Seeds, 3 Water, 4 Harvest, 5 Sprayer**. **F** is the Sell shortcut, **I** opens inventory and **F1** opens help. Farm time runs at normal speed. Accounts, conversations, loss reading and the front page pause time. Drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom, and Home to recenter. Touch has a movement stick and Tools drawer. Inventory contains potatoes by grade and seeds.

Tool upgrades widen your reach. Open another half-field for **48,000**; barn upgrades increase its initial **200-tonne** capacity. Pip hires up to two ducks to clear pests. Three local challenges pay **4,000** each and can be claimed once. These are optional purchases and goals; their costs and receipts still enter the ledger.

Seed packets, wooden workbench slots, pinned quest notes and the open ledger have different shapes. Cream highlights facts; wood and ink-green hold the page. A crop, barn, forecast or ledger drawing identifies each before its heading. Shop pages and keeper conversations arrive from the building you opened; the farm keeps moving behind them. Nell and Tess keep the guide brief, with the action and the consequence together.

See the [README](../README.md) for running or exporting the source and [development notes](DEVELOPMENT.md) for saves and tests.

Tess keeps the map’s cork board. Quests and Losses are tabs on the same board, with cream pinned notes; an ink outline marks the selected tab. Each loss note names the event and field, lost tonnes, base-price worth and one counterfactual. Losses cover the current year, so an earlier seasonal loss is never followed by an empty-season message. Her conversation and Iris’s station have no separate loss-notices entrance.

The weather station and Weather pill open Iris’s same service. Its instruments show the next-season probability bracket, event names and field exposure. Protection and irrigation tiles show levels and one build or work action. Spring insurance is one toggle. The sensor upgrade is one line; Winter frost-cover batch work remains on the jobs note.

The barn’s buyer slips show crop, tonnes, quote, Autumn-end deadline and shortfall penalty. Accept stamps the order; a question mark explains it.

The year’s front page keeps its newspaper masthead and headline on an ink-green frame, with a small cream accounts leaf. A ten-box weather strip records disaster icons, with its legend behind a question mark. When a previous year has closed, a small Accounts box prints its net. Start the year remains outside the article scroll area.

Bram’s Tools shed offers tool upgrades, barn capacity and **Open more Home beds · 48,000 · any season**. Its lease reminder points to Winter accounts. **Decorations · from 2,000** offers a bench, flower border, scarecrow, painted barn door, duck house and gate flag, priced from 2,000 to 12,000. Choose from short placement lists. These cash purchases have no bonus or timer and remain visible in the fifty-year epilogue.

Field boards print only the field’s name. A shelter, waves or hill symbol sits on the post; available fields have a **TO LET** board. World lettering is measured against the board and shrinks to fit.

Foreclosure keeps the editorial ink-green page and opens on its paper category ledger, with the real balance, overdraft limit and only applicable loss figures. The lead-in connects that final state to the farm’s future.

This development build starts a new farm when a save contains the earlier lease or business rates. The incompatible save is preserved separately rather than changing its journal.

On phones, Sell stays available during weather. The weather card’s touch area follows its visible height so farm beds and Winter’s Sleep action remain reachable.

Phones start with a **2048 shadow map**. Phones stay at or below **2048**, including when loading an older 4096 preference. Desktop Graphics retains **4096**; Smooth turns shadows off. The existing Debug panel offers **Measure a year** without unlocking test funding. Finish the guided year first, tap it once, then play until the next Winter accounts close. A result card shows the device, actual shadow size and resolution, mean and 1% low fps, and the worst frame with its season. **Copy** copies that report. Samples stay in memory and never enter the farm save.

HUD type uses three sizes: **22 px headings, 16 px body, 14 px captions** at 390 wide. Brief hints use rounded type, translucent warm paper and a small smiling potato cue that gently moves when the message changes. They pass farm input through and stay clear of controls. Menu shows your real 3D farmer turning slowly, with a small hat tip, and opens the same four appearance choices used at first launch.

Keepers and the player breathe slowly and blink every three to five seconds. Tapping gives a small squash and release stretch. Ducks waddle, stop and look toward the farmer, with a quiet quack on tap. A few petals, leaves or snowflakes drift in the appropriate season.
